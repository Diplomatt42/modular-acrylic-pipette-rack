# Materials, cleaning and optional bonding

## Important: alcohol-based cleaning

**Acrylic can craze or crack when exposed to alcohol-based cleaners, including
repeated alcohol cleaning of a dry-fit rack. This vulnerability exists even
without solvent-welded joints. For environments that regularly clean with alcohol
solutions, we strongly recommend choosing a suitable polycarbonate or PETG sheet
instead of acrylic.**

Confirm compatibility with the sheet manufacturer for your exact grade, cleaner,
concentration, contact time and use conditions. Alternatives are not universally
resistant to every disinfectant: [Plaskolite's compatibility guide](https://plaskolite.com/docs/default-source/tec/tec275_psk_disinfect.pdf)
lists alcohol-compatible TUFFAK polycarbonate and VIVAK PETG, and explains the
effects of stress and repeated exposure.

For ordinary acrylic cleaning, use mild soap, water and a soft cloth. Use only a
manufacturer-approved cleaner or disinfectant for the exact sheet. Some acrylic
grades permit specific diluted alcohol formulations; that is not approval for
routine lab-strength alcohol cleaning. See [ACRYLITE care guidance](https://www.acrylite.co/resources/fabrication-manuals/clean-disinfect-and-maintain-acrylite-acrylic-sheet).

## Changing the sheet material

The delivered CAD and validation are for nominal 3 mm acrylic. Polycarbonate and
PETG are material alternatives to evaluate, not separately validated builds here.
Measure thickness, recalibrate the joint fit, and reassess shelf deflection and
stack stability. Confirm that your fabrication process supports the exact sheet
and thickness; do not reuse acrylic laser settings automatically. Polycarbonate
can give poor CO2-laser cut results, including discoloration; consult your machine
provider or use a suitable machining process. See [Epilog's polycarbonate guidance](https://www.epiloglaser.com/how-it-works/faq/can-you-cut-engrave-polycarbonate-with-laser-machine/).

## Optional solvent welding for added stability

The rack is designed for modular, easy dry-fit assembly and flat-pack shipping.
If additional stability is needed after fitting and squaring an acrylic prototype,
compatible acrylic cement can permanently bond the panel-to-shelf seams.
Thin capillary cement may be applied with a needle applicator where edges are in
close contact. The default loose tab-slot gaps are not close-contact seams; use
a cement intended for the actual gap and follow its instructions.

Methylene chloride (dichloromethane) is an acrylic-bonding solvent, but it is
hazardous and regulated. Use it only where permitted under your lab's approved
controls and the product SDS. [EPA's guidance](https://www.epa.gov/assessing-and-managing-chemicals-under-tsca/risk-management-methylene-chloride)
describes US restrictions. Acrylic cements with other formulations are available;
see [ACRIFIX solvent cements](https://www.acrylite.co/products/brands/acrifix/solvent-cements).

[Example acrylic-cement product link supplied by the project author](https://a.co/d/034aoZOj).
Check the actual product's formulation and suitability; the link is not a tested
joint specification. Bonding prevents disassembly at the bonded joints and does
not establish a load rating or make acrylic alcohol-resistant.
